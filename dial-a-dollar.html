Why it can win

Why blockchain and voice are both necessary

PieceWhat it doesWithout it
XRPL ChecksHolds the money for María until she claims it, and lets it return to Carlos if she never does.A bank or wire company sits in the middle and takes about 6%.
Shared ledgerCarlos, María and the shop all trust one record of who paid whom, across borders and without a bank between them.Each company keeps its own database, and you have to trust it.
ElevenLabs agentLets someone with no smartphone, no app and no English send money just by talking.The people who send money home most can't use the product at all.

Ripple's main business is cross-border payments, so this is the exact problem their track exists for. The voice agent is the whole front door, so ElevenLabs is core to it, not an add-on.

Judge questions

"How does María turn this into pesos?"Through local cash-out agents, the same model M-Pesa uses. In production, licensed partners would do this. For the demo, the shop is a web page.
"Who holds María's keys?"The service holds them, tied to her phone number. That's custodial, the same as most remittance apps. A later version could let her move to her own wallet.
"What stops a stranger calling as Carlos?"Caller ID plus a spoken PIN. Large transfers could also need a callback.
"Why not Venmo?"Venmo doesn't cross borders. Wires take days and cost about 6%. This settles in seconds for under a cent, and nobody can freeze it in the middle.
